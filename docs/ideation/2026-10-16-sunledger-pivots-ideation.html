Alternatives to SunLedger

Focus: reuse the proposed stack to build a product with a different user, decision, and demonstration from peer-to-peer solar certificate trading.

Three finalists. Thirty-six suggestions were consolidated into 25 candidates, then three cross-frame combinations were added. Three of the resulting 28 candidates are recommended for deeper exploration; the other 25 are recorded below.

Grounding Context

Codebase Context

The repository was empty when inspected. There is no application code to preserve. Reuse means keeping the proposed tools and engineering concepts: React/TypeScript/ethers, Python/FastAPI, NumPy/Pandas/scikit-learn, SQLite, Solidity/OpenZeppelin, and Hardhat. Useful concepts include simulation with hidden truth, signed evidence, uncertainty checks, oracle authorization, escrow, and reproducible comparison.

The user's target is October 24, 2026. Each finalist is a bounded simulation prototype, not a production service. Exploring alternatives does not authorize implementation or replace the earlier agreed MVP. Battery modeling, different beneficiaries, and any track change require a fresh scope decision.

Novelty boundary: none is established as the first of its kind. Sources show substantial adjacent work. These are hypotheses about a specific product workflow and a demonstrable contribution. Customer demand, real deployments, and competitor internals have not been validated. Working names are provisional and have not been checked for trademarks.

  • Powerledger advertises energy trading and hourly traceability. A different household type or token format is weak differentiation.
  • CloudWatts Zonnedimmer advertises curtailment control, reconstruction and compensation. Basic curtailment payments are already a product.
  • AEMO Project EDGE studies export-envelope fairness and efficiency. Fair allocation is an existing research subject.
  • EnergyTag V2 includes storage accounting and ex-post meter correction. Generic correction or revocation is insufficient as a new product claim.
  • Allume SolShare provides solar allocation and tenant visibility; Sunreport advertises solar performance and maintenance evidence. Basic sharing and fault dashboards are crowded.

Ranking weighs a changed purchaser outcome, visible distinction, stack fit, useful multi-party enforcement, measurable results, and prototype feasibility. Confidence below is subjective confidence that the direction merits a scoped prototype, not a probability of commercial success or proof of uniqueness. Candidate evidence received uneven scrutiny; the finalists received targeted prior-art checks.

1. ReserveGuard — prevent impossible backup promises

Description: A community cooperative reserves a finite, simulated solar-backed battery for specified outage-service windows. The product checks whether the same resource has been promised to too many participating users, shows obligations that become under-backed, and settles agreed service shortfalls with test funds.

User
Community-energy cooperative arranging backup for a community freezer and cooling facility.
Axis
Incentives and disputed performance; secondary benefit is transparent distribution of scarce backup.
Basis
reasoned: an energy balance does not reveal how many obligations depend on it. A shared reservation view can expose incompatible promises before an outage. external: microgrid service-guarantee research already addresses storage planning and constrained sharing; this candidate emphasizes participant commitments, shortfall visibility, and settlement rather than inventing backup-power optimization.
Rationale
Changes the purchased outcome from certificate ownership to an inspectable time-specific service obligation. A commitment failure produces an observable consequence rather than a misleading green token.
Confidence
75% — worth a bounded prototype; market demand remains untested.
Complexity
High relative to current design: adds battery state, power limits, reserve scheduling and outage replay.

Demonstration: a resource has 3 kWh of conservatively available energy. Two loads each request 2 kWh during an overlapping outage window. Independent bookings look valid; the combined promises cannot both be honored. Show the conflict before acceptance. Then reduce availability after a valid booking, reveal the newly uncovered obligation, and execute the agreed refund from prefunded escrow.

Proposed distinguishing mechanism: availability evidence → exclusive participating reservations → under-backing detection → explicit shortfall settlement. Both kW and kWh limits matter; promises must be jointly feasible across the modeled schedule, not only pairwise checked.

AI role: optional telemetry anomaly detection can flag implausible reported availability. It cannot establish actual capacity or substitute for deterministic reservation constraints. If the competition requires a substantial AI contribution, define and evaluate that task before selecting this direction.

Blockchain role: a common commitment and escrow state for cooperating parties. It cannot discover promises outside the system, prevent physical depletion, force delivery, or guarantee that a resource can reach a load. A local chain demonstrates rules rather than public decentralization.

Possible evaluation: independent booking versus shared capacity-aware booking, using identical requests and hidden availability. Measure incompatible accepted commitments, service shortfalls, unserved simulated energy, and legitimate requests unnecessarily refused. Compare a centralized implementation of the same reservation rules as well: blockchain does not create the physical improvement.

Downsides: batteries were excluded from the original MVP; this is a real pivot. Limit the demonstration to an explicitly connected simulated system, not an arbitrary neighborhood. Renewable Energy & Energy Trading appears a plausible track, subject to organizer interpretation. Do not promise emergency safety or real-world service guarantees.

2. CoolAccess — verify that a funded cooling service was actually available

Description: a donor funds a cooling-center operator for declared heat-event hours. The product reconciles evidence that a specified room was open, within its agreed temperature target, and available at the contracted capacity. Payment follows supported service intervals; missing or conflicting evidence holds the disputed portion.

User
A community funder and cooling-center operator accounting to participating residents.
Axis
Fair distribution of climate benefits and climate evidence failure.
Basis
external: Carrier already offers performance-based cooling services, while Swiss Re describes heat-triggered support. reasoned: a weather trigger or an equipment installation does not establish that a publicly funded cooling space was open and accessible during the event.
Rationale
Different beneficiary and decision from an energy marketplace: the funder asks what service was delivered, rather than who owns renewable attributes.
Confidence
70% — clear demonstration; access evidence and funding demand need validation.
Complexity
Medium for one room and a few simulated intervals; high if expanded to real attendance or thermal optimization.

Demonstration: the heat trigger activates a six-hour agreement. During three hours the room is open and readings support the target; during one it is closed; during two the temperature evidence conflicts. The contract releases payment for the three supported hours, records the failed hour, and holds the two disputed hours under a declared deadline/refund policy. Temperature alone cannot establish that anyone attended or benefited.

Proposed distinguishing mechanism: a donor–operator–community service record combines an external heat event with corroborated availability and explicit uncertainty handling. This is narrower than inventing cooling-as-a-service or heat insurance; whether existing products already provide this exact workflow remains open.

AI role: flag implausible temperature transitions, stuck sensors, or mismatches between operating status and telemetry using separate simulated training and test scenarios. An anomaly remains a reason for review, not proof of misconduct.

Blockchain role: hold the promised funding and enforce the agreed supported/failed/disputed split with duplicate protection. Keep sensitive observations off-chain. Multiple signers do not establish independence if they collude; the oracle and evidence sources remain trust assumptions.

Possible evaluation: weather-trigger-only payout versus service-based settlement. Measure unsupported service payment, legitimate payment delay, unresolved intervals and false anomaly flags. Neither approach proves improved health outcomes from simulation.

Downsides: new service definition, privacy boundaries and evidence sources. Likely fits Climate Data & Environmental Monitoring more naturally than energy trading; confirm fit before adopting. Solar generation may support the simulated center but must not be assumed from temperature readings. It is not a clinical safety system or an official insurance product.

3. FlexLab — try to exploit an energy reward before it launches

Description: a program designer enters a proposed demand-response payment rule. Simulated households behave honestly or strategically; the workbench shows which strategies collect rewards without producing the intended benefit, then replays revised rules against the same behaviors and test-token settlement.

User
A community demand-response program designer or researcher.
Axis
Incentives and disputed performance; secondary concern is honest participants harmed by defensive rules.
Basis
external: baseline manipulation research explicitly studies strategic consumption; blockchain demand-response research already implements baselines and reward settlement. reasoned: a reproducible exploit-and-replay workbench makes a specific payout failure visible before funding a program.
Rationale
Changes the user from an electricity buyer to a mechanism designer, and the outcome from trading to rejecting a vulnerable rule. A failed design is an informative result.
Confidence
75% — strongest research fit; distinct commercial demand is untested.
Complexity
Medium if limited to two payout rules and three bounded strategies.

Demonstration: an honest household normally consumes 3 kWh during an event and cuts to 2. Another inflates historical use to 5 before reporting the same event consumption of 2. A naive rule reports reductions of 1 and 3 kWh. The simulator's separate counterfactual exposes how much reward came from baseline influence. Replay with precommitted baseline rules and an explicit recovery-window criterion, showing both reduced exploitation and any payment withheld from honest participants.

Proposed distinguishing mechanism: exploit → quantified test-token gain → rule change → identical-scenario replay → inspectable on-chain settlement. Neither demand response, its simulators, nor baseline anti-gaming is new. Differentiation depends on the integrated user workflow and the particular attack/defense experiment.

AI role: scikit-learn can estimate consumption under stated conditions using training data separated from evaluation and compare against a simple deterministic baseline. A learned model can also be gamed. Do not claim that baseline commitment alone prevents manipulation of the underlying consumption history.

Blockchain role: execute the compared payout rules against committed authorizations using test funds. Python can find the same exploits without a chain; the added value is showing actual contract behavior and externally inspectable settlement, not discovering ground truth.

Possible evaluation: attacker gain after the cost of extra consumption, unsupported rewards, legitimate rewards withheld, event peak and recovery peak. Load shifting and pre-cooling are not inherently harmful: define the intended service before labeling a behavior an exploit. Report energy changes separately from emissions; do not infer avoided carbon from kWh timing alone.

Downsides: counterfactual assumptions dominate results; an unconstrained attacker creates misleading wins. Existing research and tools are close prior art. Freeze the modeled actions and use a held-out scenario set. Renewable Energy & Energy Trading is a plausible fit, subject to organizer interpretation.
